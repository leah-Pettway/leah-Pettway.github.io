## Overview

[Across The western coast wetland areas act as the home fro solitary ground nesting bees. The health of their habitat is in danger due to the rise in anthropogenic development, so using imperviouys surfaces as a proxy for human development I coul quanitfy the amount of habitat fragmantation and human influence the areas have undergone over the course of twenty years.]

## My Role / Contribution

[I designed the workflow and analysed the surface change of the coastal wetland sites to in turn synthesize the findings with the ecological finding of my supervisor.]

## Approach

[To measure the surface change over time a bufffer of various distances were set to cover the designated area of interest. Then using a USGS remote sensing product which reported the percentage of impervious surfaces over a 30m resolution]

## Results / Outcome

[Small incremental changes within teh surfaces across locations and time suggested that the management of these coastal sites are successul in limiting the further habitat fragmentation. These results were then further presented at the UCSB URCA symposium and held at the UCSB Library to further showcase my independent research.]
